## Pilot one corridor, not the whole globe

A 30-day pilot should prove that one supplier corridor can settle with clear owners, evidence, and escalation — not that every subsidiary is ready. Scope beats theater.

## Week-by-week plan

| Week | Focus | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Owners + KYB pack | Named RACI, docs submitted |
| 2 | Policy + invoice fields | Written payment SOP |
| 3 | Dry run + tooling | Test payable packet complete |
| 4 | Live pilot invoice | Settled + reconciled |

![Team planning a payments pilot on a whiteboard](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1531403009284-440f080d1e12?w=1200&h=720&q=80&auto=format&fit=crop)

## Day-0 checklist (before marketing crypto payments)

1. Assign executive sponsor, ops owner, compliance owner, accounting owner.
2. Complete [KYB for the business account](/blog/kyb-for-business-crypto-accounts/).
3. Pick one asset/network pair suppliers already understand.
4. Update invoice templates with network, asset, and reference fields.
5. Configure maker-checker approvals.
6. Write the incident playbook for wrong address / wrong asset.
7. Agree reconciliation SLA with accounting.
8. Limit the pilot to named counterparties.
9. Define success metrics (cycle time, exception rate, packet completeness).
10. Schedule a go/no-go review on day 30.

## Risks and limits

Expanding to many corridors before controls exist multiplies incident surface. Public “we accept any coin” messaging creates support chaos and compliance noise.

## Go-live criteria

You are ready to widen only if: KYB is approved, one live invoice reconciled cleanly, exception playbooks tested, and accounting signed off on the evidence pack.
