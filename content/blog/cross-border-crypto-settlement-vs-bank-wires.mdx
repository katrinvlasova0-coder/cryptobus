## Compare rails, not slogans

Cross-border crypto settlement can be faster than correspondent wires in some corridors, especially outside banking hours. It is not automatically cheaper or simpler. The useful comparison is end-to-end: funding, conversion, transfer, confirmation, reconciliation, and exception handling.

## Speed and cutoff realities

| Dimension | Typical bank wire | Crypto settlement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Banking hours | Cutoffs and holidays matter | Chains operate continuously |
| Intermediaries | Correspondent banks | Network + VASP / desk |
| Confirmation | SWIFT + credit advice | Block confirmations + ack |
| Exceptions | Recall processes vary | Wrong address often final |

## Cost drivers to model honestly

Model: platform fees, spread on conversion, network fees, ops time, FX alternatives, and failure costs. A “cheap” hash that needs three days of reconciliation is not cheap. Pair this with [multi-currency invoice settlement](/blog/multi-currency-invoice-settlement-with-crypto/) when invoices are not denominated in a single stablecoin.

## Ops checklist before switching a corridor

1. Confirm supplier acceptance of asset + network.
2. Validate KYB status on both sides where required.
3. Define quote validity and payable SLA.
4. Align accounting evidence with current wire packs.
5. Document escalation for stuck or partial payments.
6. Pilot one corridor before wide rollout.

## Risks and limits

Restricted corridors, Travel Rule obligations, and banking partners’ risk appetite can block crypto settlement even when a chain is technically available. Volatility risk appears if you leave conversion until the last minute using non-stable assets.
