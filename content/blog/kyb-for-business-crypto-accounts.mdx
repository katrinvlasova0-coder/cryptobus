## KYB is a company process, not a form upload

Know Your Business (KYB) for a crypto account checks the legal entity, ownership, control, and expected activity. Treat it like bank onboarding: assign owners, gather documents once, and keep a refresh calendar.

## Typical document pack

1. Certificate of incorporation / extract from the company register.
2. Articles or equivalent constitutional documents.
3. Proof of registered address.
4. Beneficial ownership disclosures.
5. Directors’ / authorized signatories’ IDs as required.
6. Source-of-funds / source-of-wealth narrative for expected flows.
7. Expected corridors, assets, and monthly volumes.

| Item | Internal owner | Refresh trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registry extract | Legal / ops | Annual or on change |
| UBO list | Compliance | Ownership change |
| Signatory list | Finance | Role change |
| Activity profile | Treasury | New corridor |

## Timeline expectations

Build buffers for incomplete packs, translation needs, and follow-up questions. Parallelize: legal gathers registry docs while finance drafts activity profiles. Do not start a payable pilot that depends on an unfinished KYB.

## How KYB connects to invoice payments

Without a verified business account, you cannot reliably separate personal wallets from company settlement. That breaks audit trails and often violates policy. See also [AML controls for crypto invoice payments](/blog/aml-controls-for-crypto-invoice-payments/).

## Risks and limits

KYB approval is not a guarantee that every corridor or counterparty will be allowed later. Activity outside the declared profile can trigger reviews. Incomplete UBO disclosure is a common delay.

## Owner checklist

1. Name a KYB project owner.
2. Collect registry + UBO pack.
3. Draft expected activity memo.
4. Confirm signatories.
5. Track provider questions in one thread.
6. Store the approved pack in a controlled folder.
